Van cards: three compositions

Each card carries everything: the old facts (ATM, Tare, Length, Sleeps) and the new ones (drive-away price, suspension, water, electrical). Same three vans, same data, same type and colours. Only the composition changes. A dash means the van's listing doesn't give that figure; Sleeps isn't on any listing, so it is filled from the layout notes where they imply it (the Amaroo's queen bed plus two bunks) and left blank elsewhere. Photos repeat because the stock photos are still to come.

Hierarchy: what the eye reads first, second, third Harmony: one label style, one number style, one set of paddings, the thirds of the card

Option A

Stacked: price, then numbers, then three lines

Hierarchy: the card reads top to bottom in one clear order. The name sits on the picture, the price is the loudest figure, and the four number cells come next. The three plain-language lines sit last, as supporting detail. Harmony: every band shares the same left edge and padding, and the four number cells split the width into equal quarters. The cost: it is the tallest card.

  2. 2Price
  3. 3ATM · Tare · Length · Sleeps
  4. 4Suspension · Water · Electrical
Option B

The original card, with the new facts underneath

Hierarchy: the top of the card is the card as it has always been: photo and name on the left two thirds, the number rail on the right third. Under it, a lighter footer carries the price first, then the three lines. Harmony: it keeps the thirds layout the site already uses, so nothing the team knows changes. The cost: the price is the second thing the eye meets, not the first, and the footer has to be clearly separate so the card does not read as two cards.

  1. 1Photo + name
  2. 2ATM · Tare · Sleeps · Length (rail)
  3. 3Price
  4. 4Suspension · Water · Electrical
Option C

Price on the picture, then a two-thirds / one-third panel

Hierarchy: name and price share one baseline on the photo, so the two things a buyer looks for first are read together. Below, the panel splits on thirds: the three descriptive lines take the left two thirds, and the four numbers sit as a tidy two-by-two in the right third. Harmony: one split line, used once; lines and numbers each have their own zone, so nothing competes. It is the most compact card, but the smallest type of the three, so it leans on the card being large enough.

  1. 1Name + price (on the photo)
  2. 2Suspension · Water · Electrical
  3. 3ATM · Tare · Length · Sleeps

Data: Outbound RVs' live stock listings (Amaroo 2200, Hornet 1806, XTR). The real build would read it from the stock feed.
